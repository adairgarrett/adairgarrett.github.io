About & Research Mission

Interdisciplinary & Community-Engaged Engineering

As an aspiring academic faculty member in transportation systems engineering and urban planning, my mission is to lead interdisciplinary, community-engaged research that advances the resilience, reliability, and safety of rail and public transit networks.

Rail and transit systems form the backbone of sustainable urban mobility, yet they face increasing challenges from extreme weather events, aging infrastructure, and shifting demand patterns. My dissertation work—"Advances in Assessing and Building Resilience Capabilities for Rail Systems"—characterizes multi-dimensional adaptive capacity across physical rail assets, transit operating agencies, and riders.

By combining qualitative stakeholder engagement with quantitative modeling, structural equation modeling (SEM), and spatial vulnerability assessments, my work provides actionable frameworks for transit agencies and DOTs to make equitable, climate-ready infrastructure investments.

Transit & Urban Rail Systems

Evaluating passenger rail, light rail, and multimodal transit reliability, network connectivity, and track infrastructure adaptation under acute disruptions.

Climate Hazard & Multi-Hazard Adaptation

Developing GIS tools and hazard exposure matrices for flooding, extreme heat, and severe storms across state DOTs and urban transit agencies.

Infrastructure Asset Management

Integrating lifecycle sustainability, triple-bottom-line criteria, and workforce competencies into rail and highway asset management plans.
